Economic stress, measured against its own normal.

Each currency, energy price and food price is compared with its own past changes. An item is marked unusual only when the move is far outside that range. Fertilizers and critical-list metals

Watched countries side by side

Each country's currency and local food readings next to each other, per US dollar (a rise is a weaker currency). Daily rates are the ECB's; official monthly averages are the World Bank's Global Economic Monitor; market rates are those WFP collects in local markets, many of them unofficial. "Market against official" compares the median market rate with the official average for the same month. Nothing here is combined into a score.

Local food prices in watched countries

Median three-month change across each country's market price series. Nominal prices in local currency, from WFP. Data arrives one to three months late.

Sanctions lists

What changed between Headwind's own readings of eight official lists: the US Treasury's SDN list, the UN Security Council's consolidated list, the EU's financial sanctions list, the UK Sanctions List, France's asset-freeze register, Canada's consolidated autonomous sanctions list, Australia's consolidated list and Japan's asset-freeze list with its services-ban ship list. Most of France's entries restate EU and UN measures; its own national freezes are counted and scored apart, and its restatements are counted only. Australia's and Japan's entries under UN Security Council programs are likewise counted only, never named; their own designations are named and scored (Japan's ships by IMO number: its file gives no names). A list read for the first time sets its baseline; changes are counted from then. People, organizations and vessels added, changed and removed are named as the publisher prints them, with type, programs and the countries of their addresses and nationality (vessels with their IMO numbers); birth dates, identity documents, street addresses and reasons are not carried. The UN Security Council's list is counted only: its terms bar redistributing its content. A removed entry is no longer on the list; it is shown for the 30 days a change is kept, then goes. An entry that stays on a list and changes its name, programs or type is named as changed, and a listed vessel whose name, IMO, MMSI, flag or type changes is named before and after. The date is when Headwind saw the change, not the date of the designation. Every name of the last 30 days: data file. To report an error, or to ask that the name of someone no longer listed be taken down: support@theintelligence.company.

US export-control and screening lists

The Entity List and the other US Commerce, State and Treasury lists, read from trade.gov's Consolidated Screening List. What changed between Headwind's own readings, as counts by the address country each list gives, with the entries added, changed and removed named as the publisher prints them (US government lists). An entry that stays on a list and changes its name, programs, type or address country is named as changed. trade.gov assigns the ids of the BIS and State lists itself; an entry that comes back under a new id with the same name and type is counted once, as the same entry.

Export-control, sanctions and trade rules

Rules and proposed rules published in the US Federal Register by BIS, OFAC and USTR, USTR's notices (Section 301 actions and exclusions), BIS's and ITA's Section 232 notices, and presidential documents that are tariff actions, in the last 60 days, as they were titled.

Tariff actions and the tariff schedule

How many presidential tariff actions the Federal Register published, and how many revisions of the Harmonized Tariff Schedule the US International Trade Commission posted, in the last 28 days, each against the 27 windows of 28 days before. Unusual means a robust z of 3.5 or more and at least four documents or three revisions. Actions the White House has signed that the Federal Register does not yet carry are listed apart.

Filed for public inspection

Filings by BIS, OFAC and USTR at the Office of the Federal Register, open to the public a business day or more before they are published, kept for 30 days. HW-IND-2 is met when a BIS or USTR stay, suspension, extension or Entity List filing comes within 14 days before a date that an earlier document of the same rule, docket or investigation set, or a date the filing itself says was scheduled. It is a measurement, not a judgment of why.

US import reliance for mineral commodities

Net import reliance is imports less exports, adjusted for stock changes, as a share of US consumption. Figures are annual, from the US Geological Survey, and are copied as published.

US imports by source country, from the Census Bureau

Legal events, Headwind's second strand: the decisions of highest courts, listed as each court publishes them, with the court's own case title and number. They are not measured against a normal range and not ranked by importance: every merits decision of these courts is listed.

US Supreme Court

Every signed and per curiam opinion of this term and the last, as the Court lists them, with the Court's own case title and docket number and the decision date from the docket. Headwind does not summarize or characterize a decision; the opinion is at the link. The tariff-authority cases are tracked by docket. Not covered: orders lists (the Court publishes no feed of them) and in-chambers opinions.

UK Supreme Court

Every judgment the Supreme Court of the United Kingdom has handed down this year and last, read from the Court's own site, with the Court's own case title, case id and neutral citation and the judgment date. Appeals decided together in one judgment are listed together. Headwind does not summarize or characterize a judgment; it is at the link (the citation links the Court's PDF, the title the case page). Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0.

Coming dates

From the publishers' own schedules. US inflation and jobs dates are not covered yet.

Sources and their state

A source that failed shows its last good copy and says so. Fresh, late and stale compare a source's newest data with its own rhythm (business days for the ECB, months for FAO and WFP); read means it was read and its data carry no date to judge. A note flags a reading much smaller than usual, or a publisher date that has not moved for a long time.
